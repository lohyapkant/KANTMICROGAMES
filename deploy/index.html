REFLECTIONS

I have experienced a deep ambivalence throughout the creation of this project. On the one hand, there is genuine apprehension that people who have dedicated years to studying and mastering computer science and software development may find their hard work displaced by code generated by the ever-improving capabilities of large language models. That concern for livelihoods and craft is real and should not be overlooked.

On the other hand, this technology has been truly transformative for someone like me. Although I earned a diploma in computer science more than twenty years ago, I remained a hesitant programmer and never possessed the time or technical confidence to construct interactive games or complete software independently. For more than two decades, I held creative desires that I simply could not bring to life.

Working with modern large language models changed that entirely. It is comparable to collaborating with a tireless collective of programmers, pixel artists, and automated testers at any hour of the day or night, although on free plans the work does pause when a quota runs out. It allows rapid ideation and experimentation, while my part is to brainstorm the ideas, shape the requests, play-test the games, and decide what stays. Yet, as the illustration above demonstrates, guiding artificial intelligence toward a coherent and unified vision often produces surprising results. One game, PENGUINSLIDE, passed through nine versions across four different models. Its first version did not even show the penguin, and its second version existed only to repair a single missing comma that stopped the whole shelf from loading. The models assemble whatever parts seem to fit each request. The resulting aircraft may have propellers on the wings, rockets on the tail, and cockpits in unexpected places, but it flies.

The result is KANTMICROGAMES: twelve (and counting!) tiny pixel-art games in one file, built across more than forty numbered builds in about three weeks, on free or trial plans. The recorded model working time comes to more than twenty hours, a small amount of time beside the more than twenty years that came before it. The project is the realization of that long unfulfilled dream, brought into existence through persistence, curiosity, and the eccentric power of machine collaboration.
